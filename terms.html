1 · What the app does

The app records and organizes information about property incidents. It:

  • stores photographs, document scans, audio recordings and text you provide
  • computes a SHA-256 checksum of each file when it is captured, and compares it again when a document is produced
  • compares the device clock against elapsed time and notes when the clock appears to have moved unexpectedly
  • records a device location alongside a capture, where you have permitted it, and notes the distance from a property address you entered
  • checks an address you type against Apple's mapping service, which is the only information the app sends anywhere
  • arranges what you record chronologically and produces documents from it
  • shows checklists describing what records of a given kind commonly contain
  • shows statements about actions that commonly create legal exposure for a property owner (see section 4)
  • links to publicly available official sources, such as court self-help pages and agency contact details

4 · Statements about actions that create legal exposure

For certain incident types the app shows statements describing actions that commonly create legal exposure for a property owner — for example, that removing an occupant without a court order, changing locks, or interrupting utilities may expose the owner to liability.

Where the property is in California, most of these statements cite the California code section they rest on and link to the official text. Two of them — about confronting an occupant and about posting publicly — are practical guidance rather than legal rules, and are marked as such. For properties outside California, no citations are shown.

These statements are general information. They are not legal advice, are not tailored to your circumstances, and the cited provisions are written about tenants — whether an occupant is a tenant is frequently the question in dispute. The law varies by state, county and circumstance, and may have changed.

Do not rely on these statements in deciding what to do. Consult an attorney licensed in your jurisdiction.

7 · Checksums, timestamps and location

The app computes a SHA-256 checksum of files when they are captured and compares it when a document is produced, and again when it examines its own storage. A matching checksum indicates the file's contents have not changed since the app recorded it.

It does not establish what a file depicts, when or where it was created, who created it, or whether it is authentic in any legal sense.

Capture times are taken from the device clock, which you control and which may be inaccurate. The app notes when the clock appears to have moved unexpectedly, but cannot establish the true time of anything.

Where you have permitted location access, the app records a device location with a capture and may note its distance from an address you entered. Device location is approximate, can be inaccurate, and establishes nothing about what occurred.

We make no representation about the evidentiary weight of anything the app produces.
