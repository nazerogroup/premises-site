What it does

Tells you what to capture

Pick what's happening and the app gives you a checklist for that situation — which angles, which documents, what's worth photographing before you clean anything up.

Builds the timeline for you

Every photograph, scan, and recording lands on a dated timeline as you go. Gaps are noted rather than hidden, so the record is honest about what it doesn't cover.

Produces the right report

Four versions of the same record, arranged for whoever needs to read it: a short one for an officer on a doorstep, a full one for an attorney, one for an insurer, and one for your own files.

What it won't do

Every report ends with a page stating plainly what this app did not determine.

  • Whether a tenancy exists
  • Whether anyone is occupying a property unlawfully
  • Whether a document is genuine or legally effective
  • Whether any notice is sufficient
  • Whether a removal would be lawful
  • Whether police should take any action
  • Whether anyone has committed a crime
  • Which account, where accounts differ, is correct

Those are questions for a court, an attorney, or a peace officer. An app that answered them would be worth less, not more — a record that quietly takes a side is the first thing an opposing lawyer takes apart. Premises organizes what you have and says so.

Support

Where is my data stored?

On your iPhone. Premises has no server and no account system, so there is nowhere else for it to go. Deleting the app deletes everything permanently — nobody can recover it for you, including us.

What does the fingerprint actually prove?

That a file hasn't changed since the app recorded it. Each one gets a SHA-256 hash at the moment of capture, and every report re-checks them. It doesn't prove what a photograph shows or who took it — only that it's the same file.

Is this legal advice?

No. Premises organizes records you create and links to publicly available official sources — court self-help pages, court forms, bar association referral services. It doesn't interpret them, doesn't tell you which apply to your situation, and isn't a substitute for an attorney.

Does it work outside California?

The documentation side works anywhere — capture, fingerprinting, timelines, and reports don't depend on location. The official source links are California-specific for now. Other states are coming.

Can I use it without giving location access?

Yes. Photographs are simply recorded without coordinates. The same is true of every permission the app asks for — it keeps working with all of them off, with less in the record.
